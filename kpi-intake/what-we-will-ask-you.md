# What we will ask you and why

You have been asked to an interview about a KPI. This page says what to expect.

We keep one shared record of what each KPI means: its name, its definition, where it is used and where its data comes from. Reports, dashboards and AI assistants read that record, so they all give the same answer to "what does this number mean". To add a KPI to the record, we need facts that only the people who use the KPI can give.

A business analyst will ask you about 39 questions. The interview takes 60 to 75 minutes. You do not need to prepare a document. It helps if you bring the place where the KPI is written down today: a report, a policy or a list.

We will ask six things.

1. Which part of the business the KPI belongs to, which process it is about, and which data domain it belongs to.
2. What it means, in one or two sentences, what it counts and leaves out, and which strategic objective it serves.
3. How it is worked out in words, its unit, how often it is stated and at what grain, its status, and any thresholds.
4. What kind of number it is. For example, a market benchmark you cannot control such as a crack spread, a comparison with a plan, or a KPI with a target and an owner.
5. What other names people use for it, and which numbers people mistake it for.
6. Who uses it, who its named owner and steward are, and which data product gives it.

We will not ask for the KPI's values or for formulas in code. If it has thresholds, we note them. The record holds what a KPI means. The systems hold the numbers.

Where we can, you will choose from a list. If the right answer is not in the list, say so. It means the record lacks something, and we add it before your KPI.

After the interview, the owner of the definition reads the filled form and signs it, and the owner of the domain confirms that the KPI belongs to it. Nothing changes in the record before that.
