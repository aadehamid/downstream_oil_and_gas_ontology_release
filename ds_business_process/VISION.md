# Vision

`DS_Business_Process` exists so that any downstream oil and gas company can start from a shared, evidence-backed model of its business instead of building one from scratch.
It serves the ontologists, architects, data teams and agents who model and query refining, supply and trading, midstream and logistics, marketing and the enabling functions.
It owns exactly one thing: the company-agnostic reference layer of downstream business processes and the measures, roles, data products and systems that attach to them.
A worked company specialization, for the fictional Pearland Petroleum Corporation, ships beside it as the example of how a company layer is built, and is never read as part of the reference.

## Industry, not company

The reference describes what the downstream industry does, never how one company runs itself.
Internal systems, org units, job titles, targets, owners, named assignments and KPI designations belong to a company layer, added when the work is for one company.
Nothing internal to a company appears anywhere in the reference: no internal system, org unit, job title, target, owner or internal term, not as a label, a hidden label, a definition or an example.
Anything a company has made public may be used: a company appears as the publisher of a cited source, as a named, quoted example of a definition it published, or in the migration map.
A company concept becomes a reference concept only when it is restated in generic terms; where public companies define the same thing differently, the scope note names the cited variants.
A homegrown system becomes a generic capability with a neutral name for the function it serves; an off-the-shelf product may stay as a labelled example of that capability.
Specializing for a company adds names, targets and choices; it never redesigns the reference.

## Complete for the industry

KPI Store 1.2 is the seed, not the boundary.
Each module covers every typical item that public evidence supports, sorted into that module's kinds.
A term enters when three or more independent companies show it in public disclosures, or when a published standard defines it and the term can be tied to work that operators are shown to do.
A process framework such as APQC fills a gap only where no operator names the process but the work is evidenced, for example by a regulation, a role or a segment disclosure; a framework element that cannot be tied to operator work stays out.
Breadth comes first: a typical item may enter on search-result evidence from three or more filers, marked as such, and its full source is read before the release.
Downstream-adjacent work that three or more downstream companies run, such as crude gathering, NGL fractionation and power trading, is in scope.
Rules that exist in one jurisdiction are included and marked with that jurisdiction.
Proprietary benchmark measures are included by name, meaning and a link to their owner, without their licensed method.
Company-agnostic never means generic: refining, trading, logistics, terminal, quality and risk distinctions stay.
Where no public source covers an area, the gap is recorded as a finding, never filled by stretching a neighbouring standard.

## Evidence first, and traceable

Standards drive the design; peer disclosures validate it; one company's practice never decides it.
Every definition cites a public source with its title, publisher, URL, version and access date.
Every claim carries its evidence class: verified, triangulated, interpretation or assumption.
Standards are mapped by IRI, never copied, and imported only when small, open and otherwise rebuilt term for term.
Every 1.2 term has a traceable path through the migration map; a 1.2 term that exists only because one company works that way is excluded, not carried as deprecated.

## Meaning, and only public formulas

The ontology holds meaning: definitions, kinds, components, roles and relations.
A formula ships only when it is public and the same everywhere, such as the EIA utilization ratio or the 3-2-1 crack spread; company formulas, values, thresholds and computation live in the systems that consume the ontology.
A measure has one nature and one or more roles; its calculation links never loop, while its driver links may, each with an evidence level.
Systems appear as generic capabilities, with public off-the-shelf products only as labelled examples.

## Checked before it changes

No change lands until its check exists and has been seen to fail on a broken copy.
Every count reconciles, and a reviewer from another model family reads every change before it merges.
The released ontology passes published quality checks: OOPS!, FOOPS!, qSKOS and a reasoner.
The skills that build it improve with every gap found; their method stays generic for any industry, and DS_Business_Process appears in them only as a labelled worked example.

## Scope

It is not one company's operating model, not a data warehouse schema, not a KPI target book, and not a store of measured values.
It does not ship company formulas, proprietary benchmark methods or licensed standard text.
A change aligns when it adds or sharpens an industry-typical concept backed by three independent companies or by a standard tied to operator work, keeps 1.2 traceable, and arrives with its check.
A change should be resisted when it encodes one company's internal structure or vocabulary in any form, adds a framework element no operator work supports, rests on an inference stated as fact, copies licensed content, or lands without a check seen to fail first.
